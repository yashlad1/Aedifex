# Outreach — the words to actually send

The funnel is defined in [plans/2026-08-24-reality-sprint.md](plans/2026-08-24-reality-sprint.md):
fifteen minutes first, a sanitised sample second, [DATA_REQUEST.md](DATA_REQUEST.md) third. This file
is the missing part — what to type.

Every message here is short on purpose. The one attempt made so far was a long post that explained
the project and asked strangers for 15–20 minutes: **333 views, one reply.** That is roughly 0.3%,
and it is a fact about the method rather than about the problem.

Three rules hold across all of them.

- **Ask for one specific thing, not for a conversation.** "Can I ask you one question" outperforms
  "would you have 20 minutes to chat".
- **Do not describe Aedifex.** The moment it sounds like a pitch the reply rate collapses, and the
  answers stop being about their work.
- **Warm beats cold by an order of magnitude.** Every channel below is ordered by how much trust
  already exists, and the first two are the ones that have not been tried.

---

## 1. Northeastern faculty — the least used and most likely

Northeastern has a Construction Management programme inside Civil and Environmental Engineering, and
programmes like it run on industry advisory boards, co-op employer relationships and alumni in
exactly the roles needed here. A professor forwarding an email is worth a hundred cold DMs.

**Subject:** MSCS student — question about construction billing workflows

> Hello Professor ——,
>
> I'm an MSCS student here at Northeastern. I'm trying to understand how running account bills
> actually get checked on real projects — who prepares them, who verifies them against measurements,
> and where the manual work is.
>
> I've read the public tender documents, but I can't see the post-award side from the outside.
>
> Would you be able to point me to one person — a former student, a co-op employer, someone on an
> advisory board — who has actually done or supervised that work? A fifteen minute conversation is
> all I'm after.
>
> Thank you,
> Yash

Send this to several. Construction management, project management, and any faculty listed with
industry practice.

## 2. Family and friends in India — the highest-trust channel there is

Construction is one of the largest employers in India. Someone within two degrees knows a
contractor, a site engineer, an architect or a builder. This is a *referral* ask, not a research
ask, and it should be almost embarrassingly short.

**WhatsApp:**

> Hi ——, small favour. I'm doing a university project on how construction bills get checked on site.
> Do you know anyone who works with a builder or contractor — engineer, site supervisor, architect,
> anyone in that line? I just want to ask them how bills are prepared and checked. 15 minutes on a
> call, nothing more. Can you connect me?

Notice what it does not do: no explanation of the software, no NDA, no documents, no mention of a
company. It asks for a name.

## 3. LinkedIn, to someone who does the work

Search for **billing engineer**, **quantity surveyor**, **QS**, **project controls**, **contracts
engineer**. Filter to India if the target is the Indian market. Prefer people with 3–10 years of
experience over directors: they still open the spreadsheet themselves.

**Connection note — 300 character limit, so this is the whole message:**

> Hi ——, I'm a grad student at Northeastern researching how RA bills get checked against
> measurements on real projects. Not selling anything. Could I ask you one question about how you do
> it? Happy to keep it to a single message if that's easier.

The "single message if that's easier" line matters. It gives them a way to say yes that costs them
ninety seconds, and about half of those become a call anyway.

**If they reply, the one question is always this:**

> Thank you. Here it is: **when you checked the last RA bill, what did you actually open, and in
> what order?**
>
> I'm trying to understand the real sequence — which file first, what gets compared to what, and
> where you have to go and ask somebody else.

## 4. Reddit, reshaped

The subreddits that failed are general. Try **r/QuantitySurveying**, **r/civilengineering**,
**r/india** professional threads, and Indian construction and QS groups on LinkedIn and Facebook,
which are more active than Reddit for this industry.

And change the shape. Instead of introducing a project and requesting time, ask one question that a
practitioner enjoys answering:

> **Quantity surveyors and billing engineers: what actually goes wrong when an RA bill is checked
> against the measurement book?**
>
> Not looking for anything, just genuinely curious what the common failure is. Wrong item numbers?
> Quantities that don't tie back? Rates that changed? Something else entirely?

People who will not give you fifteen minutes will happily correct a stranger on the internet, and
the answers are the same data.

## 5. The escalation, once someone recognises the workflow

Only after they have described how they work. Never in the first message.

> This has been really useful, thank you. One more ask, and please say no if it's awkward.
>
> To test whether any of this can be checked automatically, I need a real set of documents that
> match each other — a priced BOQ, a measurement sheet, and the RA bill for the same period, from
> one project. Names, addresses and companies can all be removed, and if the money is sensitive you
> can multiply every rate by the same number and not tell me what it is; the checks all still work.
>
> A finished, closed-out project is usually the easiest to share. In return I'll send you a written
> report on that project — every discrepancy I can find and prove, with page references, and an
> honest account of what I got wrong. No charge, no obligation, and I'll delete everything on
> request.

Then, and only then, attach [DATA_REQUEST.md](DATA_REQUEST.md) or its PDF.

## Tracking

Every conversation that happens goes into
[research/CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md](research/CUSTOMER_DISCOVERY.md) using the template verbatim. A
Reddit comment or a one-line DM reply is **not** an interview and is recorded under "Signals that are
not interviews" instead — the 10, 15 and 30 checkpoints only mean something if the count stays
honest.

Ask everyone, every time: **"show me how you checked the last bill."**
